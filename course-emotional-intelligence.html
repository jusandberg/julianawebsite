Clear the air

Emotion is information

Emotional intelligence is noticing what you feel, managing it, and using it to understand yourself, connect with others and make better calls. Most people carry a few wrong ideas about it. Sort these four.

So EQ is not about being nice or hiding things. It's awareness, then choice. The EQ wheel breaks that into five areas you can work on.

Design notes

How I built this course

At a glance

Audience
New managers who are leading people through change for the first time
Format
Five-minute microlearning, practice first
Model
EQ-i 2.0, five composites
Bloom's
Remember, understand, apply

The goal

Describe emotional intelligence in plain words, name the five areas of the EQ wheel, and spot which one a real moment at work is asking for.

Why each activity looks the way it does

  1. Myth or fact. Most people arrive thinking EQ means being nice or staying calm. Clearing that up first makes room for the real definition.
  2. The wheel. Learners open each area themselves, so the five areas become a map they built rather than a list they read.
  3. Name the area. One moment per area, with the reason after every answer, so a wrong answer still teaches something.
  4. Notice, name, choose. Each wrong option names the pattern it falls into. Learners finish with a response they could actually say in the room.

How I would measure it

Level 1A one-question pulse at the end: how useful was this for a hard moment you have coming up?
Level 2Round 1 score, plus how many tries it takes to build the response.
Level 3A 30-day check-in: can each manager name one moment where they paused and chose their response?
Level 4Engagement survey items on psychological safety, compared before and after the cohort.
