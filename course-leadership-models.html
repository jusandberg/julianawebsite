Servant leadership

Robert K. Greenleaf, 1970

Your main job is to help your people grow and do their best work. Your authority is there to serve the team.

On a real team

The team keeps waiting for you before anything can move.

Ask yourself

What's getting in my team's way that I could clear?

Six leadership styles

Daniel Goleman, Harvard Business Review, 2000

Good leaders switch styles depending on the moment, the way a golfer picks a different club for each shot.

On a real team

A manager who's great in a crisis keeps running every week like it's a crisis.

Ask yourself

Which style does this moment need, and which one am I using out of habit?

Authoritative

Coaching

Leading yourself · 3 of 9

The Five Practices

James Kouzes and Barry Posner, The Leadership Challenge

They studied thousands of leaders at their best and kept finding the same five behaviours. Anyone can learn them.

On a real team

People follow what the leader does more closely than the values written on the wall.

Ask yourself

Which of these five have I let slide lately?

1

Model the way

Do what you say you'll do, and show what matters by how you act.

2

Inspire a shared vision

Paint a picture of the future people want to be part of.

3

Challenge the process

Look for better ways of working, and learn from what goes wrong.

4

Enable others to act

Build trust, share decisions and help people grow their skills.

5

Encourage the heart

Notice good work and celebrate it, in ways that feel real.

Leading the business · 4 of 9

The Golden Circle

Simon Sinek, Start With Why, 2009

People are more likely to support a change when they understand why it matters. Start with the purpose, then explain how and what.

On a real team

Strategy updates are all numbers and deadlines, and nobody remembers them a week later.

Ask yourself

Have I explained why this matters before what we need to do?

What

What you do

How

The way you do it

Why

The purpose

Most updates start with what and often leave out why
When you start with why, people understand the what

Leading change

John Kotter's 8 steps, Leading Change, 1996

Big changes fail in predictable ways, usually by skipping the early steps. Do the groundwork before you push.

On a real team

A change gets announced with lots of energy, and six months later things are back to how they were.

Ask yourself

Have we built enough urgency and support before we push this change?

Making it happen

Situational Leadership

Paul Hersey and Ken Blanchard

Match how you lead to where the person is on this task: how skilled they are and how confident they feel.

On a real team

Your strongest person goes quiet on a new kind of project, and you leave them alone like always.

Ask yourself

On this task, does this person need more direction from me, more support, or both?

S4

Delegating

Hand it over and stay available

S1

Directing

Clear steps and close follow-up

S3

Supporting

Listen, encourage, decide together

Coaching

Leading the team · 7 of 9

The GROW model

John Whitmore, Coaching for Performance

A simple shape for a coaching conversation. Instead of handing over the answer, you ask questions that help them find it.

On a real team

People keep bringing you problems, and you keep solving them yourself.

Ask yourself

What could I ask here instead of giving the answer?

G
Goal

What do you want to happen?

R
Reality

What's going on right now?

O
Options

What could you try?

W
Will

What will you do, and by when?

You ask the questions, and they work out the answer.

Leading the team · 8 of 9

The Five Dysfunctions

Patrick Lencioni, The Five Dysfunctions of a Team, 2002

Teams build from the bottom up. Without trust people avoid real debate, so they don't fully commit, and results slip.

On a real team

Meetings are polite and quiet, and decisions keep getting reopened afterwards.

Ask yourself

Where does our team crack first?

TrustAbsence of trust
Healthy conflictFear of conflict
CommitmentLack of commitment
AccountabilityAvoiding accountability
ResultsResults slip
Each layer rests on the one below it
When trust is missing, the layers above it don't hold up

How teams grow

Bruce Tuckman, 1965

New teams go through stages. A rough patch along the way is normal, and it usually means the team is working through real issues.

On a real team

A team that started well hits a bumpy few weeks, and everyone wonders what went wrong.

Ask yourself

What stage is my team in, and what do they need from me right now?

Forming

Polite, a bit unsure

Storming

Friction shows up, and that's normal

Norming

Ways of working settle in

Performing

The team runs well on its own
