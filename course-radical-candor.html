The idea

Two questions decide everything

Kim Scott found that good feedback sits at the meeting point of two things: how much you care personally about the person, and how willing you are to challenge them directly. Drop one, and feedback slides into a failure mode.

Most well-meaning managers fail on the Ruinous Empathy side: they care, so they soften the message until there's nothing left to act on. Let's train your eye.

A strong rewrite usually does three things:

  • Names the specific behaviour, not a vague "tweaks"
  • Says why it matters (the impact)
  • Stays warm and offers a clear next step

Design notes

How I built this course

At a glance

Audience
New managers who avoid hard feedback or deliver it too bluntly
Format
Five-minute microlearning, practice first
Model
Radical Candor (Kim Scott)
Bloom's
Understand, analyze, apply

The goal

Recognize the four feedback styles, tell radically candid feedback apart from the look-alikes that feel safer, and rewrite weak feedback so it lands.

Why each activity looks the way it does

  1. Two forces. Learners pull care and challenge together themselves. The point that you need both is felt before it's explained.
  2. The quadrant. Moving the dot through each corner builds a mental map of the model before any testing starts.
  3. Spot the style. Real lines managers say, with the reason after every answer, so a wrong answer still teaches something.
  4. Build the feedback. Each wrong option names the style it leans toward. Learners practise the whole model, not only the right answer, and finish with a message they could actually send.

How I would measure it

Level 1A one-question pulse at the end: how ready do you feel for your next feedback conversation?
Level 2Round 1 score, plus how many tries it takes to build the feedback.
Level 3A 30-day check-in with each manager's own leader: are they naming specific behaviour and a next step?
Level 4Engagement survey items on feedback quality, compared before and after the cohort.
