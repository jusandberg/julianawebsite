Manager Feedback Skills

Feedback that actually helps.

Most feedback fails for one of two reasons: it's too soft to be useful, or too harsh to be heard. Radical Candor is the space in between. Five minutes, three quick rounds of practice.

5 min Practice-based Apply on the job

By the end, you'll be able to

Recognize the four feedback styles, tell radically candid feedback apart from the look-alikes that feel safer, and rewrite weak feedback so it lands.

The idea

Two questions decide everything

Kim Scott found that good feedback sits at the meeting point of two things: how much you care personally about the person, and how willing you are to challenge them directly. Drop one, and feedback slides into a failure mode.

Manipulative Insincerity
Low care, low challenge. Empty praise, political silence.
Radical Candor
High care, high challenge. Honest and kind. The target.
Obnoxious Aggression
Low care, high challenge. Blunt, belittling, all edge.
Ruinous Empathy
High care, low challenge. So nice it's useless.

Most well-meaning managers fail on the Ruinous Empathy side: they care, so they soften the message until there's nothing left to act on. Let's train your eye.

Round 2 · Make it land

Rewrite it with radical candor

Here's a piece of ruinously empathetic feedback: kind, but useless. Rewrite it so it keeps the care and adds the challenge.

The weak version
"Hey, the client deck was great, really good effort! Maybe just tiny tweaks here and there next time, but honestly don't worry about it, it was fine!"

A strong rewrite usually does three things:

  • Names the specific behaviour, not a vague "tweaks"
  • Says why it matters (the impact)
  • Stays warm and offers a clear next step
One model answer "I want this deck to land because the client meeting matters for you. Two things would make it stronger: the recommendation is buried on slide 9, move it up front, and the data slides have no takeaway line, so the client has to interpret them. Want to rework those two together before Thursday?"

Nice work.

Carry this into your next 1:1

  • Care personally and challenge directly. Dropping either is the failure.
  • "Being nice" is often Ruinous Empathy in disguise. Specifics are kinder than vagueness.
  • Name the behaviour, state the impact, offer a next step.
